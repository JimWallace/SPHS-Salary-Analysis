# How the APR process plays out in salaries: three diagnostics

Generated by `scripts/apr_process_diagnostics.py`. Aggregates only; named inputs and per-person outputs are in `data/private/` (gitignored).

## Data

- Annual raise = disclosed salary in year t minus year t−1 (public UW disclosures, 2011–2025).
- Excluded raises: a person's second disclosed year (the first may be a partial year), the year of a change in disclosed rank and the year after (promotion increases straddle two calendar years). For SPHS-only analyses, years with a stipended Director, Associate Director or Associate Dean role are also excluded.
- **Residual raise** = raise minus the median raise in the same unit and year. It removes scale increases and one-off payments that everyone received (e.g. Bill 124 retroactive pay in 2023–24).
- Disclosed titles show academic rank only, never administrative roles, so stipends for Chairs and Directors in KIN and RLS cannot be removed. Cross-unit comparisons therefore apply the same rules to all three units and rely on medians.

## A. Do raises stick? (SPHS)

If the process rates the same people high or low year after year, a person's residual raise should predict their residual raise in later years. The null ("no persistence") reassigns residuals at random among the faculty observed in each year; p is the share of shuffles at least as persistent.

Sample: 187 annual raises, 29 people.

| Measure | SPHS | Expected with no persistence | p | Same, after removing the MHI/non-MHI difference | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Correlation with next year's residual raise | -0.30 (n=143) | -0.01 | 0.999 | -0.21 | 0.976 |
| Correlation two years later | +0.00 (n=108) | -0.01 | 0.460 | -0.07 | 0.665 |
| Correlation three years later (no shared rating or raise-timing overlap) | -0.03 (n=92) | +0.02 | 0.646 | -0.04 | 0.712 |
| Rank correlation: mean residual 2012–17 vs 2019–25 | +0.28 (n=16) | +0.00 | 0.156 | +0.29 | 0.140 |
| Share of raise variation that is between people (ICC) | -0.05 (n=29) | +0.03 | 0.914 | -0.04 | 0.869 |

Notes: a merit increase takes effect around May 1, so it is split across two calendar-year raises; this makes the one-year correlation partly mechanical. Since about 2019, tenured faculty are rated every second year and the rating sets two years of increases, which also links raises two years apart. The three-year correlation and the early-versus-late comparison avoid both overlaps.

## C. Does sharing research areas with the committee help?

For each person and full review cycle: the share of that cycle's committee members (excluding the person and the Director) who share at least one SPHS research-group tag with them. Tags come from the current public faculty listing, so they are a 2026 snapshot applied to all years. Outcome: the above-median two-year raise from the APR committee analysis (promotion windows and admin years excluded). p-values shuffle proximity among faculty within each cycle.

Sample: 56 person-cycles, 23 people with tags; 32 of 35 SPHS faculty matched to tags.

## Interpretation

- **Year to year, raises do not stick.** A person's above- or below-median raise in one year says almost nothing about the next few years; most year-to-year variation looks like timing and noise. Over longer stretches there is some stability: in Kinesiology, and in SPHS when administrative stipends are left in, people above the median in the earlier years tend to be above it later. With SPHS stipend years removed (section A) the long-run correlation is weaker and not significant, so part of that stability is stipends. Removing the MHI/non-MHI difference barely changes the SPHS results.
- **SPHS compresses raises more than its sister units.** In most years the spread between high and low raises is narrower in SPHS than in Kinesiology or Recreation and Leisure Studies, while the typical raise is about the same. That is what a rating process with a tight target average would produce, though stipend changes in the other units (not visible in the disclosures) could also widen their spread.
- **The MHI gap is specific to MHI, not to SPHS.** Non-MHI SPHS faculty receive raises close to the Faculty of Health median; MHI faculty sit about $1,000–2,000 a year below it, smaller than before 2021. With about five MHI faculty this difference from KIN and RLS is not statistically clear on its own.
- **Research-area overlap with the committee shows no consistent benefit.** Across people the sign is negative; within person it is positive but not significant. Informatics faculty share research groups with about as many committee members as everyone else, so the MHI gap is not explained by a lack of research-area representation (as measured by group tags).

## Caveats

- Only currently listed faculty are included in all three units, so people who left (possibly because of poor raises) are missing.
- Salaries below $100,000 are not disclosed, so early-career years are missing, more so before 2016.
- Research-group tags are a current snapshot and coarse; they do not capture methods or venues.
- Calendar-year salary mixes two merit years, which adds noise to one-year raises.
